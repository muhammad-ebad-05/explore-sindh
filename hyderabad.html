Hyderabad: The City of Perfumes, Pearl Craftsmanship, and Cultural Elegance

1. Historical Legacy and Royal Foundations

Nestled along the eastern bank of the Indus River, Hyderabad serves as the second-largest city in Sindh and the historic capital of the Kalhora and Talpur dynasties. Founded in 1768 by Mian Ghulam Shah Kalhora atop a limestone ridge, the city was built over the ancient settlement of Neroon Kot. Its magnificent historical heritage is Anchored by Pacco Qillo (The Brick Fort) and Kacho Qillo, alongside the regal tombs of the Kalhora and Talpur rulers. Historically celebrated for its unique wind-catchers (Mangh) built on rooftops to catch cool breezes, Hyderabad earned the timeless title of the City of Perfumes.

2. Artistry, Bangle Crafting, and Local Markets

Hyderabad holds global recognition for its specialized artisanal industries and vibrant street shopping culture. It is world-renowned as the center for colorful glass bangles (Kanch ki Churiya), crafted through meticulous traditional fire and glassmaking techniques. The bustling lanes of Shahi Bazaar—one of the longest outdoor retail bazaars in Asia—and Resham Gali offer an endless display of Sindhi handicrafts, traditional block-printed Ajrak, delicate embroidery, and fine jewelry. The city’s unique craftsmanship reflects a centuries-old dedication to preserving traditional Sindhi trade and fashion.

3. Educational Hub and Literary Heart

Beyond its commercial energy, Hyderabad serves as the educational and literary heartbeat of interior Sindh. Adjacent to the city lies Jamshoro, home to premier higher education institutions including the University of Sindh, Mehran University of Engineering and Technology (MUET), and Liaquat University of Medical & Health Sciences (LUMHS). The city has produced generations of legendary Sindhi writers, poets, and scholars who have enriched the region’s intellectual and Sufi literature.

4. Culinary Delights and Modern Lifestyle

The culinary culture of Hyderabad is celebrated across Pakistan for its distinct flavors and traditional recipes. Famous for the iconic Bombay Bakery cakes, spiced Rabri, and rich traditional Sindhi biryani, the city’s food culture operates deep into the night. Combining serene views along the Indus River front, historic charm, vibrant bazaars, and academic excellence, Hyderabad stands as a proud guardian of Sindhi culture and regional heritage.
